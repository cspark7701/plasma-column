Executive Overview & System Architecture

High-current ($30\text{ keV}$, $10\text{ mA}$) proton beams in compact cyclotron Low-Energy Beam Transport (LEBT) lines experience strong space-charge perveance ($K_0 \approx 6.43 \times 10^{-4}$), causing rapid beam envelope expansion, emittance growth, and heavy transmission loss at the entrance aperture of the spiral inflector.


This project evaluates a compact gas-ionized plasma neutralizer cell placed upstream of the primary solenoid lens to achieve high space-charge compensation ($\eta_{\text{net}} \ge 90\%$).


Baseline Injection Line Geometry:

buncher → plasma neutralizer → solenoid → quadrupole Q1 → quadrupole Q2 → spiral inflector

Physics Models & Ionization Kinetics

Impact Ionization Kinetics

Proton-impact collisions generate secondary electron-ion pairs:

\[ p^+ + \text{Gas} \longrightarrow p^+ + \text{Gas}^+ + e^- \]

Secondary electrons are trapped in the beam's space-charge potential well, while ions are repelled radially.

Buildup Time Constant

The space-charge neutralization time constant \(\tau\) is:

\[ \tau = \frac{1}{n_{\text{gas}} \sigma_{\text{ion}} v_{\text{beam}}} \]

Buildup kinetics: \(\eta(t) = \eta_{\text{ss}} (1 - e^{-t/\tau})\)


RF-Bunched Beam Space-Charge Perveance Limit

When an RF buncher is upstream, the beam enters the plasma cell already bunched with bunching factor \(B_f = I_{\text{peak}} / I_{\text{avg}}\). While background electrons provide average neutralization \(\eta_{\text{avg}}\), peak-bunch space charge is reduced by:

\[ \frac{K_{\text{eff,peak}}}{K_{0,\text{peak}}} \approx 1 - \frac{\eta_{\text{avg}}}{B_f} \]

Literature Review & Historical Context

  • Cyclotron Axial Lines: Early papers (Jongen 1981, Kazarinov 2007, 2010) identified severe space-charge limits in high-current injector lines. Retarding Field Analyzer (RFA) tests by Winklehner et al. (2013, 2014) showed 60%–95% neutralization in ECR LEBTs.
  • Electron Columns & Lenses: Space-charge compensation experiments at IOTA (Park 2019, 2020; Freemire 2018; Stancari 2016, 2021) demonstrated controlled electron trapping to counteract proton space charge.
  • Gabor Plasma Lenses: Palkovic (1988) tested Gabor lenses for 30 keV protons, though Nonnenmacher (2021) documented Diocotron plasma instabilities.
